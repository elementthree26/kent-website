**Naming debate — Industrial vs. Professional vs. Products:**
- "Professional" is common in the industry (P&G Professional, McCormick Professional) and typically maps to food service specifically, which could cause overlap/confusion with a separate Food Service tab.
- "Industrial" was proposed as the term Kent's own industrial customers would actually recognize and search for — if you're not an industrial customer, the word means nothing to you either way, so it self-selects correctly.
- Johnnie's read: keep the language authentic to how Kent actually talks — landed on **Food Service, Industrial, Consumer Brands** as the three tab names, deliberately avoiding invented terminology.
- Almost exclusively, "Industrial" maps to the Squincher brand (plus possibly some functional beverages, cocoa in break rooms) — kept narrow and clean rather than folding in tangential products.

## What each tab needs to say

The content in each tab should speak to the customer who lives in that world — what they're looking for, what problems they're solving, why they'd come to Kent. A "you're in the right place" moment for each.

**Industrial (Squincher):** Reference material pulled live from Squincher's own copy: job sites, factories, and plants across the country; hydration beverage of choice for "everyday heroes to keep America running"; delicious hydration solutions designed to prevent dehydration and heat-stress issues. Also surfaced: **hydration programming** — OSHA is increasingly enforcing that employers provide not just product, but a visible hydration program/protocol so employees know what actions to take, not just have access to drink mix. This "hydration safety program" angle, not just the product itself, is worth including.

**Food Service:** Reference language pulled from an older Kent site: "from soft serve and sauces to hydration solutions and more, we're here to help wow your guests and make your operation more successful" — classic and on-trend flavors, fresh ideas, easy dry mix products; ability to develop, blend, and package dry mix to precise specifications; in-house chefs who partner with back-of-house operations. Reframed benefit-first: add the "wow factor" to a menu, make the menu more diverse and flavorful, make the back-of-house more efficient. Summary: it's about products *and* recipes.

**Consumer:** This tab is genuinely just the end consumer trying to learn about Kent's brands and where to buy them — not brokers (who have their own tools/journey) and not retail buyers (who wouldn't be organically landing on this page either). Keep this tab scoped to that consumer-shopper journey only; don't invent an intermediary business persona for it.

## Tab priority and page-opening concern

Are these three equal categories, or should Consumer go first (least resistance) as its own thing, with Industrial and Food Service as tabs?

Rachel's structural take: since the tab content is its own module (effectively separate 50/50-style content per tab), she'd only break one out as a standalone module if it were meaningfully different in kind — otherwise, if it's purely a priority/ordering preference, just put Consumer first in the tab order rather than restructuring the module. Confirmed: stay with tabs, ordered **Consumer, Industrial, Food Service** (or Consumer, Food Service, Industrial), with Consumer first since it's the priority.

**Victoria's flag on why order matters here specifically:** since this page is brand new (no existing analog to base assumptions on), if a visitor sees only content relevant to one segment right at the top, they may think they've landed on the wrong page and bounce — especially on mobile, where only the very top of the page is visible at first. Resolution: put a module *before* the tabbed content that speaks to all three audiences at once, so everyone feels caught/hooked early, and then the tabs let each visitor go deeper into what's relevant to them specifically.

## Page shape agreed

Intro (speaks to all three audiences — consumer, industrial, food service) → Tabbed content (Consumer / Industrial / Food Service, each with a blurb and a linked logo grid) → some kind of video moment → something about "selling joy" → relevant news/trending content. Confirmed as a good shape — enough information without overwhelming the visitor.

**Logo grid/carousel behavior confirmed:** the logo set changes depending on which tab is active — click a different tab, the logos update to match.

**Logo color treatment — resolved:** Johnnie asked directly whether logos should be black-and-white by default and turn colorful on hover (as in some earlier designs), or always be shown in full color. Adrienne and Jeff's answer: **always colorful**, staying true to each brand's actual identity — treated as a brand-standards point, not a stylistic preference. Also noted as a nice side effect: the mix of different brand colors together (Squincher yellow, Cocoa Classic brown, etc.) creates a visually satisfying, energetic effect on its own.

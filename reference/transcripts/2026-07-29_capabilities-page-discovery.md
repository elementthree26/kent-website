## Food & Beverage (APAC)

People are inquiring about solutions, very much B2B — what we call white label or private label, for product that goes into their own stores predominantly. We work a lot with QSRs (quick service restaurants) for products that go onto their menus. We do a lot of LTOs — limited time offers, seasonal, on-trend, new flavors. Soft serve is probably our biggest, most dominant area, but beverage — both hot and cold — is a big area we'd like to expand and highlight, in terms of helping customers deliver new solutions and flavors. Everything we make out of our food and beverage site is pretty much customized, working with individual customer briefs around specific flavor profiles. For example, we're working with a tea brand in Vietnam that wanted to bring jasmine tea into soft serve — multiple iterations to deliver a true jasmine tea flavor profile, since flavor profiles change when something's cold versus hot. Lots of custom development work: we send samples, they give feedback, we do the next round. We're also quite capable of going to the customer directly — across Southeast Asia, India, up into the Middle East, and Australia — and working on-site in their stores or kitchens with flavors and ingredients we bring with us, to fine-tune concepts. It's kind of like we're doing their R&D work for them. Customers come with a fairly open brief — this is the concept — and we also push ideas, trends, and flavors we think could work for them. It goes both ways.

To sum it up: they come to us with an idea, and we do almost everything else. Particular brands have guidelines around ingredients they don't want used, and there are regulatory restrictions that differ by country — the team is well versed in those requirements and makes sure briefs are hit within those confines.

On using the jasmine tea story as a public example: keep it generic rather than naming the customer or the exact flavor — too specific from an IP perspective. Better to describe the process at a high level (a customer comes to us with an idea — adding an ingredient, developing a specific flavor — and we build a path through it) rather than listing a specific real example. A good angle: talk about helping customers get ahead of trending flavors (e.g., pistachio, ube) and deliver solutions in those trends, plus what's coming next.

Also worth adding to Food & Beverage: the ability to make functional/nutrition-based product — powder blends around fiber, protein, etc. — giving more than just a great-tasting product. That's a strong area of the retail market right now.

**Food service (sweet and savory).** Food service is definitely a focus area to highlight — both for APAC and for the U.S. culinary team. In the U.S., people tend to think of Kent as a soft-serve company, but there's a lot of savory work too — the sweet and savory capabilities both need highlighting. The U.S. has a dedicated culinary department with certified, classically trained chefs on staff who work chef-to-chef with clients (not just R&D-to-R&D) — the chefs travel internationally to assist (e.g., to the Far East, soon to Europe and Mexico). This culinary team was built about two years ago and has opened doors that were previously closed — it led to a partnership with the American Culinary Federation and access to colleges/universities to speak about the benefits of dry mix (shelf life, storage, make-as-you-need). Culinary is expected to play a bigger role going forward, since some clients run trends/R&D through test kitchens staffed by chefs rather than food scientists, and Kent needs to be able to speak the language of both. Engagement can be either Kent taking the ball and running the process, or a more collaborative chef-on-chef relationship — both models happen depending on how the client is set up.

**Insights/trend partnership.** Worth highlighting the ability to partner from an insights perspective — providing category or shopper-insight data (trends, growing subcategories, ingredients) to help clients build product ranges, on top of the physical product development. This happens in both Americas and APAC, includes frequent presentations to clients, and reinforces the "full suite of solutions" positioning.

## Pet Care (APAC)

Categories to potentially cover: cat litter, functional/dental pet treats, supplements, and bird seed.

**Cat litter.** There's some hesitation here since litter is a known challenge in private-label product — but the group agreed to include it for now (can pull it later if conversations with Heritage Row change). Positioning: Kent is Australia's leading solution provider for sustainable litter, providing sustainable clean-up solutions for pets across different substrates. Recommendation: keep this high-level and generic — don't go into different substrate types — but make sure the page uses words like "litter" and "co-manufacturing/packing" so it's discoverable by anyone searching those terms.

**Functional pet treats.** Kent has strong capability in functional dog and cat treats — dental, or more broadly functional/supplement products, with high efficacy, high quality, and "Australia made" being an important trust signal for the APAC region. This works the same way as Food & Beverage: a dedicated R&D team works with the customer's brief, has expertise in animal nutrition and the functional space specifically, and helps with product design, running trials, and scale-up. A good way to talk about it: describe the process — brief → ideation → development → trial → scale-up — since being able to run that full process (not just formulate) is what co-manufacturing partners are actually looking for. Also worth mentioning: a range of packing formats (pouches, flow wrap, canisters/tubs), and distribution across Australia plus export into parts of APAC.

**Audience for pet treats:** both brand-new startups getting a product to shelf for the first time, and larger/established brands looking to co-manufacture or test a new product line without committing to their own capability first (a comparable example cited: how Kent works with Welljoy in the US — a big brand testing a new offshoot product through Kent before deciding whether to invest in their own capability). Benefits worth calling out: reduced risk, innovation, efficiency, cost savings, nimbleness/small-batch agility, and custom formulation.

**Bird seed.** Smaller area — expertise in developing, blending, and packing customized bird seed solutions, including sourcing (e.g., from the Darling Downs region). Note: in APAC, bird care is classified under Pet, not Animal Nutrition — it's companion/wild bird (parrots, etc.), not farm/production birds. This differs from the Americas structure, where farm-type animal nutrition sits under Kent Nutrition Group; in APAC everything pet-related, including birds, sits under Pet.

**Priority order for Pet Care:** Pet treats & supplements first (grouped together — too much focus on supplements alone risks too many small, inefficient runs), then bird seed, then cat litter (litter ranked last partly because Kent is pushing hard on litter under its own brand, so there's less appetite to lean into it as a private-label story right now).

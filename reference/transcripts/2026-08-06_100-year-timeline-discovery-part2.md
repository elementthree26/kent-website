Quick refresher: using this sheet, looking at current milestones through the lens of keep, combine, or kill. The criteria: is there something we can say about this milestone that communicates to the customer what they get out of working with Kent Worldwide? If not, it's just boasting, which works in small doses but not across an entire timeline. We left off in the 1980s.

## Frostline Foods → the consumer brands origin story

**1981 — GPC acquires Frostline Foods.** This reads as a story about getting into lactose-free frozen desserts, which makes it feel critical to keep. What's the benefit to the customer here? This was Kent's first venture into sweets, not savory, not animal feed, and it's a portfolio Kent is working hard to expand today, this single 1981 acquisition is also what set up the recent Frosty Boy acquisition to make sense at all. This looks like the first hint of Kent's move into consumer brands generally (things that end up on retail shelves), which continues through Dacus Foods and Precision Pack Foods, all part of today's consumer brands business.

**Combine into one grouped story:** Frostline Foods, Precision Pack Foods, and Dacus Foods (Blue Seal Feeds is animal-side, not part of this grouping) since they're individually small acquisitions that combine into what is today a significant consumer brands portfolio. This answers a question the audience will actually ask: "so what does Kent do?" Answer: ag-based specialty ingredients, plus making and marketing consumer brands. Worth noting GPC connects here too, since GPC's MALTRIN went into soft serve as early as the 1980s (per Kent's own throwback ads), tying the ingredient business to the consumer brand story directly. Close the story with something like: Kent has been a consumer brands expert for roughly 40 years now.

## Blue Seal Feeds (1988)

Keep. This is the first time Kent acquired a company that had been doing the same work for a very long time and was well respected on the East Coast. This is also how Kent picked up many of its East Coast retail store locations, expanding its footprint. Ties into the "worldwide" theme that shows up everywhere else on the site.

## World's Best Cat Litter (1997)

Keep. This will become the consumer story, but it started with GPC, GPC scientists (with the current CEO/chairman involved) took a byproduct that would otherwise have been discarded and turned it into a natural, non-clay litter that worked better than clay. Ties back to the "making products that actually work and are better for customers" theme, plus a strong innovation angle: nobody thought it would turn into anything at the time, and people reportedly rolled their eyes at the idea, a real entrepreneurial-risk story. Corn's natural absorbency and the litter's flushability are also strong sustainability details.

**Word choice note:** avoid saying Kent "would have disposed of" the byproduct, since Kent always finds a use for things, "byproduct" is the accurate, safe term. Also worth being precise that this created a higher-margin product line.

## Mulberry Health Clinic $4M community commitment (2022) and philanthropy generally

This is where the group made a structural decision that affects several other milestones. Calling out a single donation felt odd in isolation, and a visitor overseas (e.g., in Singapore) likely won't be moved by a donation to a clinic in Muscatine specifically. Rather than either dropping philanthropy stories entirely or scattering individual donations through the chronological timeline, the group decided philanthropy deserves **its own dedicated space**, not a timeline entry.

Additional philanthropy examples raised in support of this: the **Give Litter** campaign (World's Best Cat Litter donations to animal shelters), ongoing **Kent Gives** programs (Kent Nutrition side), a **2017 donation of a disaster relief trailer to the Salvation Army** (delivered to Wisconsin, not Muscatine-specific, which helps make the case that this isn't just a hyper-local story), and the ISU Feed Mill/Grain Science Complex gift (see below, which does double duty). The business is described as "very philanthropic" overall, enough to justify bolstering this as its own category/section rather than a handful of scattered timeline mentions.

**Reference inspiration raised:** Coca-Cola's corporate site has a dedicated "People and Communities" section separate from its company history, cited as a model for how Kent might treat this.

**Idea floated by Scott (explicitly discussed as likely too advanced for now):** geolocation-based dynamic content, showing a site visitor philanthropy stories relevant to their own region (e.g., a Wisconsin visitor sees the Salvation Army trailer story, a Florida visitor sees a different regional example). Group consensus: interesting, but not worth building given Kent doesn't yet have this kind of curation/content maturity anywhere else on the site, flagged as a possible future roadmap item, not for this build.

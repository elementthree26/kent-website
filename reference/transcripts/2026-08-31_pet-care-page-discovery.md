# KENT+E3: Pet Care Landing Page Discovery — August 31, 2026

**Recording:** 48 mins (no highlights)
**Attendees:** Victoria Shaw, Jess Ferguson, Rachel Young (Element Three); Leandra, Marcelo Ginestel, Kirstin "KB" O'Brien, Scott Kramer, Johnnie Arnett (Kent)

First discovery session for the Pet Care category page. Marcelo (US pet marketing) was new to these discovery sessions, so the call opens with orientation on the site structure before getting into content, then works through cat litter, treats, supplements, and birdseed one at a time, and lands on a global-voice constraint driven by real differences between how Kent's US and Australian (KB's) pet businesses operate.

---

## Orientation

A recap of the pages being built: the Our Brands page (values plus global reach, each category including Pet Care gets a high-level blurb, with a scrolling carousel of that category's brands), a Capabilities page (about custom private label and co-manufacturing work, where Pet Care shows up in the context of treats, supplements, birdseed, litter, and packaging formats), and then the individual category pages themselves (Animal Nutrition, Food & Beverage, GPC), shown briefly as structural references, hero module setting up the page story, tabbed modules for multiple categories of things, full-width text for a single story/values/history moment, video and news modules as available.

**Action item surfaced here:** the Capabilities page currently lists "flexible packaging formats" as its own category alongside treats, supplements, birdseed, and litter. KB flagged this as the wrong level, packaging format is a layer underneath the pet categories, not a category itself. Agreed: remove it from the Capabilities page's category list and let it show up on the Pet Care page instead.

## Who is this page actually for?

Marcelo raised this directly and it shaped a lot of the rest of the call: should the page speak to customers (retailers like Petco/Walmart, or B2B customers seeking co-manufacturing contracts) or to end consumers? Kirstin added a third lens, her own B2B customers pursuing co-manufacturing contracts specifically.

Jess's read: this is mostly a customer-facing (B2B/retail-partner) page, since that's who's actually visiting the Kent Worldwide site, but explicitly left it open for a consumer-facing story if one felt necessary, and framed it as Kent's page to shape, not a rule Jess was imposing.

## Cat litter (established first, sets the tone for the whole page)

KB's framing: World's Best is the bigger brand internationally, but domestically Breeders Choice is Kent's leading pet brand with 30%+ market share. What connects both brands, and part of why Kent acquired the business in the first place, is that both were first to market with their specific approach: Breeders Choice (recycled paper, "hot air and water") and World's Best (corn-based). The connecting theme KB proposed: **"doing what's right for people, planet, and pets."**

Explicit language guidance: avoid leaning hard into "sustainability" as the headline word, it reads cold, and more importantly Kent doesn't have the full documentation/certifications some competitors can point to, so overclaiming here creates real exposure. Marcelo agreed and pushed to always pair the words **"natural and sustainable"** together rather than using "sustainable" alone, natural (plant-based sourcing) is the safer, still-true framing.

Scope check: is "doing what's right for people, planet, and pets" a litter-specific story or bigger than that? Both KB and Marcelo confirmed it's the umbrella for all of Pet Care, litter is just where it's most concrete (specific substrate decisions), but it should be positioned as Kent's overall approach/opener for the page, not filed under litter alone.

Additional litter content to include: the range of substrates Kent offers (corn via World's Best, paper via Breeders Choice, and mineral litter, which is unique to the Australian market), framed as giving customers options. An emotional/legacy angle: litter (and pet care generally) has been part of Kent for a long time, roughly 35+ years for World's Best specifically (unconfirmed exact number). A leadership/innovation angle: both World's Best and Breeders Choice were category-first innovators, worth saying directly rather than implying.

## Page hierarchy

Confirmed order, prompted by Leandra questioning whether treats/supplements should lead over birdseed: **Cat Litter, then Pet Treats, then Supplements, then Birdseed.** Treats and Supplements were explicitly kept as separate sections rather than grouped together, KB's reasoning: grouping them risks treats reading as "frivolous" when Kent also makes functional treats, and supplements are a genuinely different format/category from treats.

## Birdseed

Key points KB wants covered: localized sourcing (in Australia, product is packed on the Darling Downs, close to where the grain/seed is actually grown), knowledge of local wildlife, ingredient quality, longevity (Australia: 30+ years; US: potentially 50+ years, unconfirmed for Kent Nutrition Group specifically), and global footprint, birdseed is made or in-progress across Australia, the US, and soon Europe, which is worth calling out as a specific point of scale.

**Action item:** confirm the actual longevity numbers across US, Australia, and KNG, and lead the copy with whichever region has the longest-standing claim rather than defaulting to one region's number.

## Pet Treats

Should cover the full range from training/reward/connection treats through functional treats (with actives), and call out dental specifically since it's a large enough sub-category to deserve its own mention. Should note the page covers both cat and dog. Quality points to include: ability to run small batch production (a real differentiator vs. larger manufacturers, supports a "test and learn" growth partnership model, willing to start small with a customer and scale with them), and a range of packaging formats (pouches, flow wrap, canisters, etc.). Explicitly agreed not to enumerate every packaging type by name, and not to get into ingredient-level detail (meat vs. starches) beyond a high-level mention that ingredients vary across formulas. Export capability was explicitly flagged as not ready to talk about yet, still being worked through internally.

**Naming correction (important):** Marcelo caught that "flexible packaging" as a phrase implies one specific type of packaging (which isn't accurate since the US only has one option), when what's actually meant is having *multiple* packaging options available, batch size and ingredient flexibility as well as packaging. Resolved language: **"packaging flexibility"**, not "flexible packaging."

## Supplements

Similar territory to treats: functional focus, multiple formats/packaging, small batch capability, quality of ingredients. KB raised a nuance: in Australia, Kent effectively produces the majority of the supplement category, both its own market-leading brand and (implicitly) private-label work for others. In the US, Marcelo clarified, Kent's business model is different, Kent only sells its own branded supplements and treats and does not manufacture for other brands there. This creates a real risk of overpromising or misleading language if the page implies broad co-manufacturing capability that isn't actually available in every region.

## The global-voice resolution

This became the central strategic decision of the call. Marcelo asked directly where Australia sits in the page's hierarchy, is it the core of the story with the US as a secondary market, or the reverse? Confirmed: this is a single **global** page, so it can't favor one region's business model as the default. Kirstin's framing: leaving the language broad enough that it invites inquiries is actually fine, even upside, since a US-based inquiry about co-manufacturing (something not currently offered there) surfaces real competitive intel and a possible future business conversation, not just a support problem.

**Resolution (Jess's recap, confirmed by the group):** use a global voice, stay high-level and broad enough to sound like a unified global company, and deliberately avoid getting specific about what capability is available in which country, so the page never makes a promise it can't keep in a given region.

## Trust, safety, and what not to say

Jess raised a broader concern: pet food/pet care as a category has relatively low consumer trust and unclear regulatory expectations, is there a "what we don't do" or compliance/standards angle worth adding? KB's answer: stay away from specific regulatory or accreditation claims, since requirements differ meaningfully country to country (Australia and New Zealand alone differ, let alone the US, Canada, and broader Asia), and Kent doesn't have a single credential that covers every market. Safe alternative language: something to the effect of being compliant with relevant regulations, without specifying which ones, plus general credibility/trust/longevity language, which is safe and true everywhere.

## The emotional layer

KB pushed back on the page reading too "cold and functional." The real emotional throughline across every category: connection between pet and owner, not features in isolation. Examples: a well-performing litter lets an owner be closer to their pet (easier cleanup, less friction); dental treats freshen breath, again enabling closeness; functional treats/supplements addressing skin, coat, and itchiness improve quality of life for the pet directly. The page should be functional in substance but never read as purely clinical.

KB also proposed that the "people, pets, planet" framing could bookend the page, opening with it as the philosophical frame, and closing with a section on what Kent actually *does* for animal welfare beyond the products themselves, citing real examples: partnerships with animal welfare/shelter organizations in Australia (Sydney Cats and Dogs Home, Animal Welfare League, Corumbene were named), and the US-side Give Litter shelter donation campaign. Jess and Marcelo tentatively explored splitting "natural and sustainable" out from the "caring for animals" angle into two different moments on the page, but Marcelo pushed back that natural and sustainable shouldn't be separated into a standalone claim at all, it should always travel together as "natural and sustainable," not surfaced alone.

## Proposed value props (grid/common-themes module)

Modeled on a "things in common across the category" module used on other pages that wasn't used for Food & Beverage. Instinctive first pass at four value props, discussed live: quality, longevity/experience, options/variety/flexibility (packaging, batch size, formats), and (added by Marcelo) leading, recognizable brands, world-class/flagship brands like World's Best and Breeders Choice that a prospective partner would already know and trust, framed as evidence Kent is a credible, established supplier, not an unknown quantity.

**Important language guardrail on "longevity":** Kirstin flagged this explicitly and firmly, when talking about longevity in the context of functional treats or supplements specifically, the word must clearly refer to Kent's own longevity/heritage as a business, never anything that could be read as implying a promise about a pet's own lifespan. Flagged as a real legal/compliance risk if the copy is loose here.

## Resources for the copywriter

Marcelo and KB pointed to: the individual pet brand sites (Welljoy, Whistler, World's Best, Back to Nature, Breeders Choice), the KenPet website (dated, but usable as a resource), and specifically the **FibreCycle website** (Kent's pet business's name before the Kent rebrand), still live at time of the call, which KB noted has good existing language around "people, pets, planet" and longevity that's worth mining directly. Whistler was called out as the best starting point specifically for Australian bird-category content.

## Wrap-up

Confirmed page shape at a high level: open with the "doing what's right" philosophy, move into the four category sections (cat litter, treats, supplements, birdseed) each with its own high-level story, a value props module (leading brands, longevity/experience, quality, options), and close on the emotional/animal-welfare/community giving angle. Jess to draft from available resources and flag specific language gaps back to the group during review rather than guessing.
